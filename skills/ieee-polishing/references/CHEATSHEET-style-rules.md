# IEEE House-Style Polishing Cheatsheet

Source basis: IEEE Editorial Style Manual for Authors, March 2025 revision, paraphrased for this skill.

## Voice And Claims

- Prefer direct, precise sentences over promotional language.
- Keep passive constructions where they help methods read objectively, but avoid chains of weak passive clauses.
- Do not strengthen claims from "may," "can," or "is associated with" into causal statements without author evidence.
- Keep limitations visible when the original text includes them.

## Abbreviations

- Define an abbreviation at first use unless it is a standard unit or universally known in the target field.
- Use the abbreviation consistently after definition.
- Do not introduce an abbreviation that appears only once.
- Query the author when two expansions are plausible.

## Numbers, Units, And Symbols

- Use SI spacing in text, such as `5 ms`, `10 GHz`, and `3 dB`.
- Keep percent signs close to numerals, such as `18%`.
- Use consistent range notation and do not mix hyphen, word, and dash styles in the same paragraph.
- Keep variables in math style and unit symbols upright.

## Figure, Table, And Equation Callouts

- Use `Fig. N` for figure citations in running text.
- Use `Table I`, `Table II`, and equivalent roman-numeral labels when the manuscript uses IEEE table numbering.
- Cite equations as `(1)` and avoid phrases such as "Equation number one".
- Ensure first citations occur in numeric order.

## Titles And Headings

- Keep headings concise and technical.
- Use consistent capitalization inside the manuscript.
- Avoid claims of novelty or superiority in headings unless the paper establishes the claim.
